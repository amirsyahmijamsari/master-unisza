# Abstract

**TITLE: L-MOMENT BASED FLOOD FREQUENCY MODELLING OF ANNUAL MAXIMUM AND DAILY RAINFALL DATA IN TERENGGANU**

**Introduction:** Flood frequency analysis is essential for water resource management, hydraulic infrastructure design, and disaster risk reduction, particularly in tropical regions prone to recurrent monsoon floods. Conventional flood frequency analysis relies primarily on the Annual Maximum Series (AMS) framework paired with standard probability distributions formulated for temperate regions. In tropical monsoon climates such as Terengganu, Malaysia, this conventional approach causes information loss by discarding other extreme rainfall events occurring within the same year. Furthermore, standard distributions fail to model such intense tropical storms. Consequently, this study applies the robust L-moments method to address these limitations by comparing the traditional AMS approach with Complete Time-series Analysis (CTA) using daily rainfall data across 20 hydrological stations in Terengganu.

**Methodology:** The research procedure involved four phases. First, daily rainfall datasets from 20 gauging stations were compiled and processed into both AMS and full daily time-series records. Second, sample L-moments (λ₁, λ₂) and L-moment ratios (L-skewness τ₃ and L-kurtosis τ₄) were computed to characterize regional data properties. Third, nine candidate probability distributions (Gumbel, Normal, Exponential, Generalized Extreme Value, Generalized Logistic, Generalized Normal, Generalized Pareto, Pearson Type III, and 4-Parameter Kappa) were fitted via L-moments. Distribution fit was evaluated using the Mean Absolute Deviation Index (MADI) and Mean Squared Deviation Index (MSDI) against Gringorten plotting positions. Finally, design rainfall quantiles were estimated for return periods between 2 and 100 years, and an overestimation factor was formulated to quantify the recurrence bias between AMS and daily rainfall series.

**Results:** The empirical analysis demonstrated that the 4-Parameter Kappa distribution provided the optimal fit across both datasets, yielding the best fit for 40% of AMS stations and 100% of daily stations, which confirms its suitability for skewed tropical rainfall. Daily rainfall records exhibited higher L-skewness (τ₃ ≈ 0.47–0.55) relative to the AMS series, confirming positive skewness that standard models underestimate. The traditional AMS approach exhibited systematic overestimation of return periods, yielding an average overestimation factor of 8.50 at the 99th percentile relative to daily rainfall data. This discrepancy demonstrates that an extreme rainfall event classified under AMS as a 1-year recurrence event occurs approximately every 43 days when evaluated using complete daily observations.

**Conclusion:** The findings demonstrate that traditional AMS approaches misjudge the recurrence frequency of extreme rainfall in tropical environments due to annual data reduction. Flood frequency analysis in tropical monsoon regions improves when utilizing complete daily rainfall series coupled with the 4-Parameter Kappa distribution. These results provide practical guidance for hydraulic infrastructure design and flood hazard mitigation in Peninsular Malaysia.
