# Abstract

**TITLE: L-MOMENT-BASED FLOOD FREQUENCY MODELLING OF ANNUAL MAXIMUM AND DAILY RAINFALL DATA IN TERENGGANU**

**Introduction:** Flood frequency analysis is essential for water resource management, hydraulic infrastructure design, and disaster risk reduction, particularly in tropical regions prone to recurrent monsoon floods. Conventional flood frequency analysis relies primarily on the Annual Maximum Series (AMS) framework paired with standard probability distributions formulated for temperate regions. In tropical monsoon climates such as Terengganu, Malaysia, this conventional approach causes information loss by discarding secondary extreme rainfall events occurring within the same year. Furthermore, standard probability distributions may inadequately model the characteristics of intense tropical rainfall events. Therefore, this study evaluates the robust L-moments method to address these limitations by comparing the conventional AMS approach with an adapted Complete Time-Series Analysis (CTA) framework using daily rainfall data across 20 hydrological stations in Terengganu.

**Methodology:** The research procedure involved four phases. First, daily rainfall datasets from 20 gauging stations in Terengganu were compiled into both the AMS and complete daily time series for non-decimated frequency evaluation. Second, sample L-moments (λ₁, λ₂) and L-moment ratios, namely L-skewness (τ₃) and L-kurtosis (τ₄), were computed to characterize regional data properties. Third, the parameters for nine candidate probability distributions (Gumbel, Normal, Exponential, Generalized Extreme Value, Generalized Logistic, Generalized Normal, Generalized Pareto, Pearson Type III, and 4-Parameter Kappa) were estimated using L-moments. Distribution fit was evaluated using the Mean Absolute Deviation Index (MADI) and Mean Squared Deviation Index (MSDI) against Gringorten plotting positions. Finally, design rainfall quantiles were estimated for return periods ranging from 2 to 100 years, and an overestimation factor was formulated to quantify the recurrence difference between the AMS and complete daily rainfall series.

**Results:** The empirical analysis demonstrated that the 4-Parameter Kappa distribution provided the optimal fit across both datasets, achieving the best fit for 40% of stations under AMS and 100% of stations for daily rainfall series, confirming its suitability for skewed tropical rainfall. The daily rainfall series exhibited higher L-skewness (τ₃ ≈ 0.47–0.55) relative to AMS records, confirming pronounced positive skewness that standard models underestimate. The conventional AMS approach exhibited systematic overestimation of return periods, with an average overestimation factor of 8.50 at the 99th percentile relative to the complete daily series. This discrepancy indicates that an extreme rainfall event classified under AMS as a 1-year recurrence event occurs approximately every 43 days when evaluated using complete daily observations.

**Conclusion:** The findings demonstrate that conventional AMS approaches misrepresent the recurrence frequency of extreme rainfall in tropical environments due to the reduction of annual data to a single maximum value. Flood frequency analysis in tropical monsoon regions is improved by adopting complete daily time series rather than decimated annual maxima, coupled with the flexible 4-Parameter Kappa distribution. These findings provide practical guidance for hydraulic infrastructure design and flood hazard mitigation in tropical regions.
